Do provincial transfers follow need?

Buenos Aires province: 135 municipalities (partidos), 23,845 inhabited census tracts. Census 2022, transfers 2025, public health facilities 2025.

Key takeaways

Need sits below the level where money is allocated. 83% of the variation in deprivation across census tracts is within partidos, not between them. Partido averages hide most of where need is.

Transfers do not follow need. They are concentrated in the less deprived partidos. Greater Buenos Aires receives about 190,000 pesos per person in 2025, against 348,000 in the rest of the province, despite higher deprivation. Municipal size and hospital provision explain most of the difference.

Hospitals, not primary care, are the gap. Primary care centres are close to deprived tracts, but public general hospitals are farther from them: in Greater Buenos Aires, 10.8% of people in the most deprived fifth of tracts live more than 5 km from one, against 1.5% in the least deprived.

Sewer networks are missing where deprivation is. In Greater Buenos Aires, 87% of households in the least deprived fifth of tracts are connected to the public sewer, against 28% in the most deprived fifth. Transfers are not related to where the sewer deficit is.

Why health facilities are on the map

Municipalities receive a fixed share of provincial revenue (coparticipación, Ley 10.559). 37% of that share is allocated on health indicators: available beds, consultations, patient-days, discharges and facilities with inpatient care. Municipalities that run more hospitals receive more money, whatever their level of deprivation, so the location of hospitals shapes where transfers go.

What the measures mean

Unmet Basic Needs (NBI): a household is counted if it has at least one of: more than three people per room; inadequate housing (e.g. a shack or a room in a tenement); no toilet; a child aged 6–12 not attending school; or four or more people per employed member with a head who did not complete primary school. It measures structural deprivation, not income.
Current-resource deprivation (IPMH): INDEC's material deprivation index, households that cannot cover current needs; closer to income poverty.
Needs-weighted rule: the same total pool split 50% by population and 50% by households with NBI. An illustration of distance from need, not a policy proposal.
